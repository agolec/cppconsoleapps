		5.1		The Increment and Decrement Operators

	++ and -- are operators that add or subtract 1 from their operands.

		To INCREMENT a value means to increase it by one, and to DECREMENT a value is to subtract by one.
		Both of the following statements increment the variable 'num' by one:

			num = num + 1;
			num += 1;

		And 'num' is decremented in both of the following statements:
			
			num = num - 1;
			num -= 1;

		C++ provides a set of simple urnary operators designed just for incrementing or decrementing variables.
		The increment operator is ++, and the decrement operator is --. The following statement uses the ++
		operator to increment num:

			num++;

		and the following, decrements 'num' by one:
			
			num--;

		These examples above are the ++ and -- operators used in POSTFIX mode (the operator comes after the variable.)

		There is also a prefix mode, ++num and --num;

		In statements that do more than just increment or decrement a value, like

			num = 4;
			cout << num++;

		cout is doing two things: 1) displaying the value of num, and 2) incrementing num by one. But which one happens
		first is determined by whether post or prefix are used. If ++num is used, it will add 1 to num first,
		and output 5. If postfix is used, it will display 4, and increment it to 5 after cout displays its output.
